Writing Exercise 2

In response to "The Coming Age of Calm Technology"

This article was really interesting to me because of how it predicted the idea of the smart home but also the sort of analog forces that move against the proliferation of such technology in the modern day. Since the piece was written smart objects have become much more commonplace in our homes and it can even be possible to forget all the ways in which it integrates into our daily lives. However I don't think that the idea of a true smart home has reached the vision that the author of this article had believed it would even today, and I would argue that's at least partially because there are cultural ideas of maintaining simplicity and manual/analog elements in the home. I think these ideas are also influenced by the desire for privacy especially with how smart objects often encroach on the sanctity of our spaces. Given this almost lack or slowed adoption of ubiquitous computing in the present day, I found the articles ideas on calm technology to be especially interesting and relevant. Today we find technology in the forms of social media and advertising to be incredibly intrusive and attention-hungry, and there is a real counter-drive towards unobtrusiveness in the applications and technology we use. The examples given were also very thought provoking, the inner office window was one that I thought was interesting because my initial impression of the idea was that it was quite depressing, but after understanding its connection to ideas of the periphery it ended up changing my opinion on the concept.
